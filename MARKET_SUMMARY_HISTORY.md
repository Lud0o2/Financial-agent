## 2026-08-14 — Daily market lesson

TODAY'S MARKET QUESTION
Why did weak retail sales lower short-term yields while the 10-year yield, oil and energy stocks rose?

Data cutoff: about 13:00 ET; Friday's US session is still open.

REACTION MAP
• Consumption → growth: July retail sales fell 0.6%, their largest decline since May 2025. The GDP-linked control group fell 0.4%; sales excluding autos and gasoline fell 0.2%. That weakens the near-term consumption impulse and reduces the need for another Fed hike, explaining lower short Treasury yields.

• Signal quality: autos fell 1.8% and online sales 2.2% after earlier temporary boosts, while restaurants gained 0.5%. Some weakness may be calendar payback, not a new recession trend.

• Yield curve → competing risks: normally weak sales should lower yields across maturities. Instead, the 10-year rose to 4.69% from 4.63%, while long-bond ETF TLT fell 0.83%. Markets reduced near-term Fed-hike risk but demanded more compensation for long-run inflation and uncertainty.

• Inflation and oil → the long end: August consumer sentiment fell to 51 from 55.2; one-year inflation expectations edged up to 4.3%, while five-year expectations held at 3.3%. Brent rebounded 1.1% to about $88. Sticky inflation fears plus oil pressure help explain why long yields resisted the weak-growth signal.

• Equity transmission: SPY fell 0.26%, QQQ 0.40% and semiconductors 0.89%, while small caps gained 0.23% and energy 1.56%. Higher long yields hurt long-duration technology; higher oil supports energy. Small-cap resilience may reflect easier expected short-term financing, but one session cannot prove that mechanism.

WHAT THE DIVERGENCES SAY
This was not a simple “bad data → lower rates → higher stocks” day. The front end heard weaker growth; the long end heard sticky inflation and term risk. Credit barely weakened (HYG -0.11%), so this looks more like a repricing of the growth/inflation mix than broad funding stress. Gold rose 0.82% despite higher long yields, reversing Thursday's relationship and suggesting hedging demand mattered more today than its usual rate sensitivity.

LEARN ONE THING
The yield curve is not one interest rate. Its front end mainly prices the near-term central-bank path; the long end also prices inflation, nominal growth and term premium. Short yields down while long yields rise is a curve twist: policy expectations are easing, but long-horizon confidence is not improving.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE READ
If long yields retreat while credit and small caps hold, weak data is being absorbed mainly as policy relief. If oil, inflation expectations and the 10-year keep rising while HYG weakens, the stagflation interpretation gains weight. Next week's retailer earnings will test calendar payback versus deeper household fatigue.

Sources: US Commerce data reported by AP, 14 Aug 2026; AP market report; University of Michigan data reported by Axios; Yahoo Finance intraday snapshot.

## 2026-08-15 — Daily market lesson

TODAY'S MARKET QUESTION
Why did softer inflation produce only modest weekly index gains?

Markets are closed Saturday. All prices below are Friday closes; no weekend price action is implied.

REACTION MAP
• Inflation → policy relief: July CPI and headline PPI cooled, reducing the perceived need for tighter Fed policy. Yields eased and technology rallied midweek. Yet the S&P 500 gained only 0.4% for the week and the Nasdaq 0.1%: relief moved prices, but did not create durable acceleration.

• Growth → the cost of easier policy: Friday's 0.6% retail-sales decline made lower short-rate expectations less purely positive. Easier policy caused by disinflation supports valuations; easier policy caused by fading demand also threatens revenues. Friday's S&P -0.2% and Nasdaq -0.3% show the second interpretation gaining weight.

• Oil and rates → incomplete disinflation: Brent rose 1.7% Friday to $88.52 and the 10-year yield climbed to 4.69% from 4.63%. Textbook weak demand should pull long yields down. Their rise instead suggests oil-linked inflation and long-horizon uncertainty offset the growth slowdown. That kept pressure on expensive, long-duration equities.

• Breadth → a different winner: the Russell 2000 gained 1.1% for the week, beating the S&P and Nasdaq, while the Dow lost 0.6%. Small-cap outperformance is consistent with relief about future financing costs, but the Dow's weakness and the Nasdaq's flat week deny a clean broad-rally signal.

• Expectations → muted upside: the S&P completed a third winning week near a record. When favorable inflation news produces only +0.4%, the reaction is evidence: some relief was likely priced, while valuation and oil limited further re-rating. This is inference, not proof of positioning.

WHAT THE DIVERGENCES SAY
The week did not resolve growth versus inflation. Small caps heard possible policy relief; the 10-year and oil rejected full disinflation; large-cap indexes resisted higher valuations. Verified Saturday credit evidence is unavailable, so no funding-stress claim is justified.

LEARN ONE THING
Markets trade the expectations gap, not the adjective attached to a release. “Good” inflation data can produce a small gain when investors already expected relief; “bad” growth data can hurt even if it lowers expected rates. Compare the size and persistence of the reaction with what the news should normally cause.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE READ
Tuesday's import-price release and Home Depot results test whether oil and housing costs are still passing through. Target and Lowe's report Wednesday, followed by FOMC minutes; Walmart reports Thursday. Falling long yields plus improving breadth would validate policy relief. Firm oil and yields alongside weak retailer guidance would strengthen the margin-and-demand squeeze interpretation.

Sources: AP final market reports, 14-15 Aug 2026; US BLS release calendar; Federal Reserve calendar; Home Depot investor relations.

## 2026-08-16 — Weekly market map

Weekly Market Map — 10-14 August 2026

What the market learned

Inflation improved, but not enough to settle the growth-versus-inflation debate. July CPI rose 0.1% month on month and 3.4% year on year; core CPI was 0.2% and 2.5%. Headline PPI was unchanged and slowed to 4.7% year on year, but the measure excluding food, energy and trade services still rose 0.4% on the month and 4.7% annually. Friday then added a different problem: retail sales fell 0.6%, including a 0.4% decline in the GDP-linked control group.

How the reaction travelled

Rates: softer inflation initially lowered yields and supported duration-sensitive technology. FRED shows the 2-year yield moving from 4.19% on 7 August to 4.15% on 13 August. Yet the 10-year rose from 4.63% Thursday to 4.69% Friday as Brent gained 1.7% to $88.52. Near-term Fed pressure eased, but the long end still priced inflation and term uncertainty.

Equities: the S&P 500 gained only 0.4% for the week and the Nasdaq 0.1%, despite strong midweek reactions to CPI and PPI. The Russell 2000 gained 1.1%, while the Dow lost 0.6%. Small caps therefore broadened participation, but the major indexes did not confirm a synchronized growth upgrade.

Credit and volatility: US high-yield spreads moved only from 2.70% to 2.71% through Thursday, while VIX fell from 14.90 to 14.63. Weak growth data caused repricing, not a broad funding alarm. The 10-year inflation breakeven nevertheless rose from 2.25% to 2.27%, contradicting a complete disinflation story.

Earnings and flows: Applied Materials fell 5.1% despite beating profit and revenue expectations because its stock had already more than doubled this year. Reddit rose 12.6% after S&P 500 inclusion was announced. One move reflected an expectations hurdle; the other reflected anticipated passive demand.

The important disagreement

Normally, weak retail sales plus softer inflation should pull yields down and lift equities. Instead, oil and the 10-year rose while large-cap indexes fell Friday. The market is distinguishing policy relief caused by disinflation from policy relief caused by weaker demand: the first helps valuations, while the second threatens future cash flows.

Concept to keep

Markets trade the expectations gap. When favorable news produces only a small gain, the muted reaction is evidence that relief was already priced or offset elsewhere. Price response is part of the data.

Next week's tests

Tuesday brings import prices, industrial production and Home Depot; Target and Lowe's report Wednesday before the July FOMC minutes; Walmart reports Thursday. Falling long yields with stable credit and sustained breadth would support constructive policy relief. Firm oil, rising breakevens and weak retailer guidance would strengthen the margin-and-demand squeeze interpretation.
